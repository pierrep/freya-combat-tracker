# Freya Combat Tracker

A local desktop combat tracker for fifth edition (2024 rules, SRD 5.2.1), built with C++20 and Qt 6 Widgets. It runs offline, and keeps everything in JSON files in your app data folder.

## What it does

**Characters.** Type a character in, or import a D&D Beyond "Export to PDF" (form fields are read; page text is not). The sheet holds HP, AC, speed, ability scores, save and skill proficiencies, classes with Hit Point Dice, spells, spell slots (with Pact Magic slots that return on a Short Rest), gear, damage resistances, immunities, and vulnerabilities, and an Exhaustion level. Initiative and proficiency bonus are worked out from the sheet; tick *Override* when a feat or feature changes them. **Short rest** spends Hit Point Dice and restores Pact Magic. **Long rest** restores HP, slots, and Hit Point Dice, clears temporary HP, and removes one Exhaustion level.

**Monsters.** The SRD 5.2.1 catalog (330 stat blocks) with search and type and CR filters. Each action carries its attack bonus or save DC, its damage dice and types, recharge, X/Day uses, and whether it is part of Multiattack. Traits, bonus actions, and reactions carry recharge and X/Day uses too (Legendary Resistance gets a Use button that counts down) (the Basilisk's Petrifying Gaze, a Mage's Protective Magic), so their Use buttons keep the tally. Stat blocks also list saving throws, damage and condition immunities, resistances, vulnerabilities, legendary action uses, and XP. **Copy as custom** makes an editable copy; custom monsters can have actions, traits, bonus actions, reactions, and legendary actions of their own.

**Custom monsters play like SRD ones.** Type or paste an entry's stat-block wording and its effects are read from it as you type: the conditions it gives ("If the target is a Medium or smaller creature, it has the Grappled condition (escape DC 12)"), how long they last, save-ends and second-failure wording, "While Grappled" ties, ongoing damage, required targets ("one Frightened creature"), Hit Point limits, and Hit Point maximum drains. A bonus action, reaction, or legendary action with a save or an attack roll is aimed at targets like an action. **Read from text** on an action also fills its attack bonus, save, typed damage, recharge, and X/Day. **Effects…** on any entry opens an editor for all of it, plus what the entry does to the monster itself (a Vanish-style condition with what ends it) and, on a trait, an aura. Once you edit an entry's effects by hand, later text changes leave them alone. A summary under each entry, and on the stat card, says what will happen in a fight.

**Parties and adventures.** On the Characters page, **Parties** makes named groups of characters (a character can be in several): **New**, a name, and a tick for each member. In Encounter Builder, **Add party** puts every member into the encounter, and until its fight starts the encounter follows the party: a character who joins the party is added, one who leaves is taken out (one removed from that encounter by hand stays out, and **Add character** still adds a single guest). Once combat starts, its characters stay as they are; deleting a party leaves its characters in their encounters. An **adventure** groups encounters: Encounter Builder's **Adventure** card makes one, names it, and picks the party its new encounters start with; its list then shows only that adventure's encounters, and each encounter's **Adventure** choice moves it. The Dashboard's adventure dropdown (shown once there is an adventure) lists only that adventure's encounters, and both pages remember the choice. Deleting an adventure keeps its encounters, with no adventure. They are kept in `campaign.json` beside `encounters.json`.

**Encounter Builder.** Create encounters, add characters (once each) and monsters (several at a time), and see the 2024 XP budget (Low / Moderate / High) for the party's levels. **Reset** puts monsters back to full HP with no conditions, recharges, or daily uses spent, clears every character's conditions, sets everyone's initiative back to 0 (characters' HP stays; one at 0 HP stays Unconscious), and restarts in the initiative phase: the Dashboard opens on its initiative list. A character's HP in the roster always follows the sheet.

**Options.** One initiative roll per monster type, whether a monster's turn passes once its action is spent, whether dice are thrown across the Dashboard, and the theme: Match system (the default) follows the computer's light or dark setting as it changes, or pick Light or Dark. Saved in `options.ini`. **Saved data** shows the folder the app keeps its files in: **Change folder...** picks another (the existing files are copied there unless it already has its own), and **Reset to default** goes back to the list below. The change applies after a restart. `options.ini` always stays in the default folder, since it holds the choice.

**Dashboard (the fight).**
- The app opens on the encounter you used last, on the Dashboard and in Encounter Builder.
- A new or reset encounter starts in the **initiative phase**, with an initiative list beside the turn order: type each character's total, or press **Roll player initiative** (d20 + each sheet's bonus), and press **Roll monster initiative** (optionally one roll per monster type); nobody has a turn yet. Start combat asks first if a character has no total or the monsters haven't rolled. **Start combat** begins round 1 with the first creature and runs its start of turn, so its auras, death saves, and durations are handled like every later turn. A monster's Exhaustion lowers its roll.
- The log under the turn order gives up its height when the turn order is long, down to three lines, so more of the order shows; with a short order it keeps its usual size.
- The **turn order** lists initiative (its heading a small d20), name, AC (its heading on a small shield), HP, and status; the Status column takes the spare width. Whoever's turn it is has their initiative in a filled chip and their name in bold. The dead (a monster at 0 HP, a character who failed three death saves) sit under a line at the end of the list, greyed, with no initiative and Dead as their only status; they can still be selected, healed, or targeted. The selected creature's card, with its Armor Class drawn inside a shield beside its name, sits beside it with Actions, Conditions, and Details tabs, and **Remove from fight** at the end of the tab row. The Details tab has Initiative (with the bonus), then for monsters Action Info (the Attack rolls choice, the Action, Bonus action, and Reaction boxes with the attacks left, and legendary actions left), then Ability Scores, Defenses, and Spell slots, each under a line. Hit Point boxes take up to 9999.
- **Next turn** ends the current turn and starts the next one: conditions with a duration count down, save-ends conditions ask for a save, a monster's action, bonus action, reaction, and legendary uses come back, expended recharge actions roll a d6, and a dying character is asked for a death save. To go back a turn, press **Undo**: it puts the turn marker back and takes back everything the turn change did. Undo and the log survive closing the app.
- A monster's actions are buttons, in a column down the left of the Actions tab with each entry's text beside them, so they line up and long text never pushes them off the card. A button names what it does (**Attack**, **Target…**, **Targets…**, **Help…**, **Use**) or why it can't (**Recharging**, **None left**); uses left today and a legendary action's cost show under it. Traits have no button. A bonus action that "takes the Disengage or Hide action" (Nimble Escape, Cunning Action) gets a button for each, with its rules underneath: **Dash** and **Disengage** show in the Status column until the end of the turn, and **Hide** asks for a DC 15 Dexterity (Stealth) check at the top (rolled with the stat block's Stealth, or its Dexterity), and on a success the monster is Invisible from Hide. Multiattack has no button of its own: the first use of an action it names takes the Multiattack action, which then allows that many uses of the actions it names, each only as often as it names it (a Vampire Spawn's two Claws and one Bite). Each of those actions shows how many are left in Multiattack under its button. **Attack** waits for a target click, rolls d20 + bonus against the target's AC, and on a hit rolls the damage — dice doubled on a critical hit, and a melee hit on a Paralyzed or Unconscious target is critical. Save effects roll damage once; click each target to roll its save (full, half, or none). Area effects (a breath weapon, Fireball) take several targets: click each creature to pick it, and click it again to take it back; nothing is rolled, not even the damage, until you click **End**, which rolls the damage once and the saves for everyone picked. Escape cancels the picks.
- **Attack rolls: Automatic** (the default) gives Advantage or Disadvantage from the rules — a Prone, Restrained, Paralyzed, or Invisible target, a Poisoned, Frightened, or Invisible attacker — and the log says why ("advantage: Aria is Prone"). Traits count too. Bloodied Fury and Bloodied Frenzy (while Bloodied), Blood Frenzy (against a creature missing Hit Points), and the Flesh Golem's Aversion to Fire (after Fire damage, until the end of its next turn) are automatic. Traits that depend on things the app cannot see are tick boxes on the monster's Actions tab: Pack Tactics (for the next attack), Sunlight and Light Sensitivity (stay ticked), and a Hobgoblin Captain's Aura of Authority on its allies (gone while the captain is Incapacitated). When Advantage and Disadvantage both apply, the log says they cancel. **Straight roll**, **With Advantage**, and **With Disadvantage** force it, for things the app doesn't track (cover, a spell).
- **"Or" and extra damage** are worked out when the app can see the reason: a swarm's weaker bites while it is Bloodied, the Blood Hawk's bigger beak against a Bloodied target, the Mimic's bite against a creature it is grappling, the Chimera's bite with Advantage. When only you can know, the action shows a tick box named for it ("If the boar moved 20+ feet straight toward it immediately before the hit: extra 1d6 piercing"); actions without such damage show nothing.
- **Dice.** Every die the app rolls (attacks, damage, saves, initiative, recharge) is thrown across the Dashboard as a 3D d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, or d20 that tumbles, bounces, and settles showing what it rolled, with a card saying what the roll was for, over a dimmed page. An attack's card says whether it hits and how the total was reached ("14 + 4 = 18 vs AC 15", or "14 (higher of 14 and 7) + 4 − 2 Exhaustion = 16 vs AC 15" with Advantage and Exhaustion); on a hit the damage dice follow a second later, and their card gives the total and the calculation behind it ("Damage: 5 slashing", "1d6+2 slashing"). No card lists the dice (they are on the table): a save's card says Success or Fail with its sum against the DC ("9 + 3 = 12 vs DC 14", "Fails automatically" when Paralyzed and the like), a death save's against 10, a Hide or escape check's with its skill ("12 + 4 Stealth = 16 vs DC 15"), a breath weapon or other save-based damage shows the total damage and its calculation ("Damage: 59 fire", "17d6 fire"), the Dice tray shows the total and its roll ("2d6+3"), and initiative has no card. **Dice sounds** (Options): every contact is heard, played from recordings of real dice in the sounds folder (see [data/sounds/README.md](data/sounds/README.md)): a die clatters each time it lands, bounces, or tumbles onto a new corner, clicks when it knocks another die, and tocks as it tips onto its face. Each hit plays the closest recording (the same die, kind of hit and force where there is one, the nearest otherwise), a different take each time with a little variation in pitch, louder the harder it lands, and left or right where the die is on the page; a kind of hit with nothing recorded is silent. Choose a Wooden table or a Felt dice tray, and the volume; **Reload recordings** reads new files without a restart, and **Play test throw** (with Loop) plays a short throw of a d4, a d6 and a d20. They stay until you click anywhere (or press Escape, Space, or Enter), which takes them away at once, even mid-throw; that click goes no further. **Dice** on the Turn order opens a tray to roll by hand: click d4–d20 or d% to add dice, set a modifier, and **Roll** (an empty tray rolls a d20); the total goes in the log. Options can turn the throwing off.
- **Damage** and **Heal** on the Turn order act on every selected creature (Ctrl-click or Shift-click to select several): amount, damage type, and Critical, with a "saved (half)" tick per creature for spells like Fireball. One undo step. The HP box also takes a change: type `-7` or `+5` and press Enter.
- **Help has its own cursor.** Harm uses the sword; healing and other help use a green sparkle (also on the Heal button). A helpful action's button reads **Pick a creature**: the Frost Giant's War Cry gives the creature you click (the giant itself too) its Temporary Hit Points and Advantage on attack rolls until the start of the giant's next turn, and the Unicorn's Shimmering Shield gives Temporary Hit Points and +2 AC until the end of its next turn. Temporary Hit Points never replace higher ones. The turn order's status shows "Advantage (War Cry)" or "+2 AC (Shimmering Shield)" while it lasts, and Automatic attack rolls use that Advantage. Characters also have a **Heal** action: type the healing the player rolled, click Heal, and click who gets it.
- **Rampage** (Giant Hyena, Gnoll Warrior) becomes usable the moment the monster damages a creature that was already Bloodied; the log says so. Using it gives the monster one more Bite (or Rend), and the turn waits for that attack before passing on. Any bonus action, reaction, or legendary action that "makes one … attack" (Pounce, Onslaught) allows that attack once the same way.
- Characters have an **Attack** action: type the damage the player rolled, click Attack, then click the target. No attack roll is made; the target's defenses still apply.
- Damage applies resistances, immunities, and vulnerabilities by type, and temporary HP first. A concentrating creature that takes damage gets a Constitution save prompt (DC half the damage, 10 to 30).
- **Bloodied** (half its Hit Points or fewer) is not a condition, so it is not on the Conditions tab. It shows as a tag beside Temp HP on the combatant's card, as an orange HP bar in the turn order, and in the log when a hit makes a creature Bloodied. Rampage, Bloodied Fury, and "or" damage that depends on it use it automatically.
- A character at 0 HP is dying and **stays in the turn order**. Damage at 0 adds a death save failure (two on a critical hit); damage of at least the HP maximum kills outright. Three successes stabilize, three failures kill, a 20 wakes them with 1 HP, and healing resets the counters. The death save prompt has **Critical Success** and **Critical Failure** for a 20 or a 1 rolled at the table.
- Each condition on a creature is a coloured tag on its card, under the HP and above the tabs (Poisoned green, Frightened purple, Grappled brown, and so on). Click a tag to see its rules at the top of the Conditions tab, with its cause and duration ("Vile Appearance", "until the end of the Hag's next turn") highlighted under its name; its × removes it. New conditions are added under **Add Condition** on that tab.
- A stat block that "casts Charm Person" (the Vampire's Charm) is aimed like an action: Humanoids only, a Wisdom save against the spell save DC, and Charmed on a failure. Tick "the caster or its allies are fighting it" for the target's Advantage. Damage ends it, except the Vampire's own Bite.
- An aura for creatures that "can see" the monster (the Sea Hag's Vile Appearance) skips a Blinded or Unconscious creature; one that needs hearing skips a Deafened or Unconscious one.
- Conditions can last until removed, until the start or end of someone's turn (for one or more turns), and can end on a save at the end of the creature's turn. Incapacitating conditions end concentration; Paralyzed, Petrified, Stunned, and Unconscious fail Strength and Dexterity saves. Exhaustion is picked from a list on the Conditions tab, with what the level does; it gives −2 per level to the d20 rolls the app makes, and level 6 kills. In the Add row, the first list sets how long a condition lasts; pick "until the start of" or "until the end of" and two more boxes appear, naming whose turn ends it and after how many of those turns, so "until the end of · Gnoll Warrior's turn · 1 turn" means it goes when the Gnoll Warrior next finishes a turn.
- A condition remembers what caused it, and that decides what ends it early. Invisible from **Hide** ends on an attack roll (after the roll, which still has Advantage), an effect that forces a saving throw such as a breath weapon, or a spell with a Verbal component. From the **Invisibility spell** it needs Concentration and ends on an attack roll, dealing damage, or casting any spell. Pick the cause when adding Invisible by hand (or "until removed").
- Abilities that give their user a condition apply it when used: the Imp's, Quasit's, Sprite's, and Oni's Invisibility and the Blue Dragon's Cloaked Flight (the Invisibility spell), and the Will-o'-Wisp's Vanish (Concentration on Vanish; ends on an attack roll or Consume Life). The Invisible Stalker starts every fight Invisible, and nothing ends it. Ending a concentration removes the conditions that needed it, and removing such a condition ends its concentration.
- **Auras** (the Sea Hag's Vile Appearance, the Ghast's and Hezrou's Stench, the Pit Fiend's Fear Aura, the Gibbering Mouther's Gibbering) ask at the start of each creature's turn, allies included (the Pit Fiend's only asks its enemies), whether it is in range. Roll the save there or enter the result: a failure applies the condition until the start of its next turn (Gibbering rolls its d8), a success can make it immune for the rest of the fight, and Not affected skips it. Illusory Appearance switches the Sea Hag's aura off; each aura has a Switch on / Switch off link on the monster's Actions tab.
- An aura skips creatures it cannot affect: the wrong creature type (Vile Appearance only affects a Beast or Humanoid; characters count as Humanoid) or immunity to its condition.
- Some actions limit their target: Death Glare needs a Frightened creature, the Succubus's Draining Kiss a Charmed one, Trample a Prone one, and the Chuul's, Glabrezu's, Kraken's, and Otyugh's grappling actions and every Swallow a creature **that monster** is grappling; Engulf needs a Medium or smaller one. Clicking another target is refused and nothing is spent. On a failed save, Death Glare drops a target with 20 HP or fewer to 0, and the Solar's Slaying Bow kills one with 100 or fewer.
- **Conditions from actions are applied for you.** A hit or a failed save gives the conditions the stat block says (about 200 SRD actions, bonus actions, and legendary actions): Prone, Grappled with its escape DC, Poisoned until the start of the monster's next turn, Frightened until the end of the target's next turn, and so on, each logged with its cause and how long it lasts. Size limits ("Large or smaller") and creature types ("isn't an Undead or elf") are checked against the target. Attacks whose hit forces a save (a Ghoul's Claw) roll that save, and "failure by 5 or more" is worked out from the roll.
- Only what the app cannot know is asked: "Did the boar move 20+ feet straight toward Aria?" (Yes also rolls the extra charge damage; ticking the action's box before the attack answers it in advance). A Darkmantle's cover checks whether the attack had Advantage.
- A **grapple** remembers who is grappling. It ends when the grappler is Incapacitated, dies, or leaves, and conditions tied to it ("Restrained until the grapple ends", "While Grappled") end with it. Attacks with Advantage against a creature the monster is grappling (the Ankheg's Bite) get it automatically.
- **Roll save**: on the Conditions tab, a condition with a repeat save shows a "Roll save" button. It asks the save now, and a success ends the condition. Spells that name their own trigger (Fear: the creature ends its turn without line of sight to you) are not asked at the end of every turn; you press the button when it happens. **Sight and hearing:** an Unconscious creature (or a Blinded one for sight, Deafened for hearing) is not given a condition that works through them: Fear, Command, Hypnotic Pattern, Color Spray, Dissonant Whispers, Vicious Mockery, and stat-block effects that say "can see" or "can hear". The log says so.
- **Save-ends conditions that get worse** (the Medusa's and Basilisk's gaze, the Cockatrice, Gorgon, and brass and silver dragon breath): a failed repeat save turns Restrained into Petrified, Incapacitated into Unconscious or Paralyzed.
- **Ongoing damage** is rolled and applied at the start of the right turn: a swallowed creature's acid at the start of the swallower's turn, the Pit Fiend's poison and a whirlwind's thunder at the start of the target's. Conditions that end "if it takes damage" (magical sleep, the Incubus's Nightmare) end when it does.
- **Spells that keep hurting.** Acid Arrow (on a hit) and Vitriolic Sphere (on a failed save) deal their later acid at the end of the target's next turn, rolled and applied for you. Ensnaring Strike's 1d6 Piercing comes at the start of each of the target's turns while it is Restrained, and Heat Metal's burn needs no save. A creature concentrating on a spell it can use again gets a **Concentrating on …** section on its Actions tab, gone when the concentration ends: a Magic action for Call Lightning's next bolt, moving Moonbeam, Vampiric Touch, Sunbeam, Eyebite, and Flame Blade; a bonus action for Heat Metal, Spiritual Weapon, Flaming Sphere's ram, the Arcane Hand's Clenched Fist, and the Arcane Sword. Areas that hurt a creature entering them or ending its turn there (Spirit Guardians, Cloudkill, Moonbeam, Flaming Sphere, Wall of Fire, Wall of Thorns, Wall of Ice, Insect Plague, Incendiary Cloud, Blade Barrier, Black Tentacles) have a button that costs no action, for whenever that happens. Each is rolled at the level the spell was cast with. **Repeat saves that hurt:** Searing Smite's extra damage comes with the hit; the target is then Burning, takes the damage again at the start of each of its turns, and is asked a Constitution save right after (a success ends it). Phantasmal Killer's failed save gives Phantasmal Fear (Disadvantage on attack rolls, named in the attack's reasons) and asks a Wisdom save at the end of each of its turns: a failure takes the damage again, a success ends it.
- **Hit Point maximum drains** (Wraith, Specter, Wight, Succubus, Vampire, Vampire Spawn, Clay Golem) lower the target's maximum by the damage taken (of the named type); the Vampire's bite heals it that much, and a maximum of 0 kills. The reduction stays with the fight until the monsters are reset.
- Bonus actions, reactions, and legendary actions with a save or an attack roll are aimed like actions (the Medusa's Petrifying Gaze, a Dragon's Banish or Thunderclap, the Chain Devil's Unnerving Gaze), with their recharge, uses, and legendary cost spent on the first target.
- A bonus action, reaction, or legendary action that casts one of several spells has a row for each, sharing its uses: a Mage's Protective Magic is **Counterspell** and **Shield**, a Priest's Divine Aid is Bless, Dispel Magic, Healing Word, and Lesser Restoration, and the Drider's Magic of the Spider Queen is Darkness, Faerie Fire, and Web. A spell the app has rules for is aimed as usual; any other is a **Cast** button that spends the ability (and starts its Concentration) and logs it. **Counterspell** makes the caster roll a Constitution save against the monster's spell save DC, and the log says what a failure means. **Cure Wounds** and **Healing Word** heal their dice plus the monster's spellcasting modifier (worked out from its spell save DC). **Shield** gives +5 AC until the start of the monster's next turn: when an attack hits a creature that has Shield ready and the roll would miss against its AC + 5, you are asked whether it casts Shield before any damage (Cast turns the hit into a miss); a player's hit asks too, with "still hits" for a roll that beats the new AC; and Magic Missile does no damage to a shielded creature.
- Every change goes on an undo stack (50 steps), including removing a combatant. A log lists what happened.
- A character's current HP, temporary HP, Exhaustion, and spell slots are the sheet's and are written back. Conditions, concentration, death saves, and the action economy belong to the fight.

## Layout

| Path | Target | Depends on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `src/core` | `combat_core` (static library): rules, dice, encounters, catalogs | C++ standard library only |
| `src/data` | `combat_data` (static library): JSON stores, PDF import | `combat_core`, nlohmann/json (private), qpdf |
| `src/ui` | `combat_ui` (static library) and `combat_app` (`freya-combat-tracker`) | `combat_core`, `combat_data`, Qt 6 Widgets |

| `tests/ui` | `ui_tests` | Drives the real pages offscreen |
| `tools/build_srd_monsters.py` | Rebuilds `data/srd/monsters.json` | Python 3 and a 5e-srd-api checkout |
| `tools/convert_monsters.py` | Converts a monster list in the dnd-data format into a `custom-monsters.json` | Python 3 (uses `build_srd_monsters.py`) |

## Build

Requirements: CMake 3.25+, Ninja, a C++20 compiler (GCC 13+, Clang 17+, Apple Clang, or MSVC 2022), Qt 6.4+ (6.8+ recommended), and qpdf (Apache-2.0). CMake looks for qpdf in `$HOME/opt/qpdf` and then on the default search path. nlohmann/json is used from the system if CMake finds it, otherwise fetched at configure time. Dice sounds need the Qt Multimedia module of the same Qt (the `qtmultimedia` component in the Qt installer, or your distribution's Qt 6 Multimedia development package); CMake reports "Dice sounds: on" or "off" when it configures, and without it the app builds and runs silently. A build directory configured before Qt Multimedia was installed remembers it as missing: delete its `Qt6Multimedia_DIR` entry from `CMakeCache.txt` (or configure a fresh directory) to pick it up.

```sh
cmake -S . -B build -G Ninja -DCMAKE_PREFIX_PATH=/path/to/Qt/6.8.3/gcc_64
cmake --build build
ctest --test-dir build --output-on-failure      # ui_tests runs with QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen
./build/src/ui/freya-combat-tracker
```

`-DCOMBAT_TRACKER_BUILD_APP=OFF` builds only the Qt-free libraries and tests. Warnings are errors by default; `-DCOMBAT_TRACKER_WARNINGS_AS_ERRORS=OFF` relaxes that.

`cmake --install build --prefix <dir>` installs the executable to `<dir>/bin` and the SRD files to `<dir>/share/freya-combat-tracker/srd`. The app looks for the SRD folder in `$FREYA_SRD_DIR`, then `../share/freya-combat-tracker/srd` and `srd` beside the executable, then the source tree it was built from.

On Linux the install also adds a menu entry (`share/applications/freya-combat-tracker.desktop`) and the app icon in the hicolor theme (`share/icons/hicolor/<size>/apps/`). `cmake --install build --prefix ~/.local` puts it in your own menu; `~/.local/bin` must be on `PATH` for the entry to launch. The window icon is compiled into the app, so it shows even without installing. The PNGs are rendered from `data/icons/*.svg` by `tools/render_icons.py` (needs Python Playwright; set `CHROMIUM` to use a specific browser).

## Saved data

Files live in the per-user app data folder:

- macOS: `~/Library/Application Support/CombatTracker/`
- Linux: `$XDG_DATA_HOME/combat-tracker/` (or `~/.local/share/combat-tracker/`)
- Windows: `%APPDATA%\CombatTracker\`

| File | Version | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `characters.json` | 4 | Versions 1–3 open. Typed initiative and proficiency bonuses become overrides only when they differ from the sheet. Old sheet conditions and death saves are dropped (the fight owns them). The first save copies the older file to `characters.v<N>.json`. |
| `encounters.json` | 4 | Versions 1–3 open; an Invisible from version 3 or earlier counts as Hide's. Each monster row keeps a copy of its stat block, so catalog edits never change a fight in progress; older rows get one from the catalog when opened. |
| `options.ini` | — | The Options page's choices, and the encounter last used so the app opens on it again (Qt INI format). |
| `history.json` | 1 | The Dashboard's undo steps (up to 50), log, and open checks, written when you leave the Dashboard or quit and read when it next opens. Steps for an encounter changed elsewhere since, or all of them if a character sheet changed, are dropped. A missing or damaged file just means no history. |
| `custom-monsters.json` | 2 | Version 1 opens. A row that clashes with an SRD id is skipped, reported, and written back unchanged on the next save. |

Edits are saved by writing a temporary file and renaming it over the old one; typing is saved after a short pause and always before you switch pages or close the window. A file that cannot be read is reported and left untouched. Negative hit points written by older builds load as 0.

## Importing monsters

`tools/convert_monsters.py` turns a monster list in the [dnd-data](https://github.com/nick-aschenbach/dnd-data) format (a JSON array of `name`, `description`, `properties`, `publisher`, `book`) into a custom monster file. Only convert material you have the right to use: your own homebrew, openly licensed content, or books you own for your own table.

```sh
python3 tools/convert_monsters.py monsters.json --list-books                  # what is in the file
python3 tools/convert_monsters.py monsters.json -o converted.json --book "My Homebrew" --report notes.txt
python3 tools/convert_monsters.py monsters.json --merge-into ~/.local/share/combat-tracker/custom-monsters.json --book "My Homebrew"
```

Close the app before merging; `--merge-into` keeps the old file as `.bak`, and converting a monster again updates the copy made last time. Stats come from `properties` or, failing that, the stat-block prose; traits, actions, bonus actions, reactions, and legendary actions are split out of the prose (or taken from the `data-Traits` / `data-Actions` / `data-Legendary Actions` lists). Their attack bonuses, saves, damage, recharge, X/Day, Multiattack counts, conditions, and attack-roll modifiers are read with `build_srd_monsters.py`'s readers; 2014 wording ("Melee Weapon Attack: +5 to hit", "must succeed on a DC 13 Constitution saving throw or be poisoned for 1 minute") is reworded to 2024 wording for reading, and kept as written unless `--modern-text` is given. Ids never clash with the SRD catalog (a same-named monster gets the book added: `goblin-warrior-my-homebrew`). Entries without AC, Hit Points, or ability scores are skipped, and lair actions and regional effects are left out. Wording the readers cannot follow (second failures, swallowing, conditional resistances such as "from nonmagical attacks") is listed by `--report`; fix those in the app with **Effects…**. Other options: `--publisher`, `--exclude-book`, `--name REGEX`, and `--skip-srd-names`.

Many entries in the dnd-data file have no stats at all, only their text. `--fill-from-open5e` looks those up in [Open5e](https://open5e.com), which serves only openly licensed monsters (the SRD, Kobold Press's Tome of Beasts 1–3, Creature Codex and Deep Magic, Level Up's Monstrous Menagerie, and a few more): an entry is filled when Open5e has a monster of the same name from the same book (`--open5e-any-source` drops the book check), and then Open5e's traits and actions are used too. The list is downloaded once to `~/.cache/freya-combat-tracker/open5e-monsters.json` (`--open5e-cache`, `--refresh-open5e`). `--allow-incomplete` keeps the rest: AC and Hit Points estimated from the CR (the middle of the DMG's monster-creation table), ability scores of 10, and a **Missing Stats** trait listing what to replace.

```sh
python3 tools/convert_monsters.py monsters.json -o converted.json --fill-from-open5e --allow-incomplete --report notes.txt
```

## SRD data

`data/srd/*.json` is SRD 5.2.1 material (CC-BY-4.0; see `data/srd/ATTRIBUTION.txt`). `monsters.json` is schemaVersion 2. `tools/build_srd_monsters.py` produced its structured fields from the stat-block text, and took damage and condition immunities, resistances, vulnerabilities, saving throws, skills, and XP (and `spells.json` its spell components) from the 2024 data in [5e-bits/5e-srd-api](https://github.com/5e-bits/5e-srd-api/tree/main/packages/5e-database/src/2024/en) (`packages/5e-database`, MIT), which is built from the same SRD. (The separate 5e-bits/5e-database repository is deprecated.) Only that folder is needed:

```sh
git clone --depth 1 --filter=blob:none --sparse https://github.com/5e-bits/5e-srd-api.git
git -C 5e-srd-api sparse-checkout set packages/5e-database/src/2024/en
python3 tools/build_srd_monsters.py data/srd/monsters.json 5e-srd-api/packages/5e-database/src/2024/en/5e-SRD-Monsters.json
```

An ability that gives its user a condition carries a `selfEffect` (`condition`, `source`, `concentration`, `endsOn`), written by hand in the script's `SELF_EFFECTS` table. `endsOn` lists `attackRoll`, `saveEffect`, `verbalSpell`, `anySpell`, `dealsDamage`, or `action:<name>`.

An action's conditions are `riders`: each lists `conditions`, `on` (`hit`, `failure`, `failureBy5`, `zeroHp`), and optionally `targetMaxSize`, `targetMaxHp`, `exceptTypes`, `ask` (a yes-or-no question, or `@advantage`), `refundDamage`, `escapeDc`, `until` (`sourceStart`, `sourceEnd`, `targetStart`, `targetEnd`, `targetThisTurn`, `minute`), `tiedTo`, `saveEnds`, `endsOn` (`takesDamage`, `sourceGone`), `worsensTo`, `worseSaveEnds`, `worseEndsOn`, `ongoing` damage with `ongoingAt` (`target` or `source`), `removes`, and `stabilize`. An attack whose hit forces a save has `riderSave`; a drain has `drain` (`type`, `heals`); `advantageIfGrappled` gives Advantage against a creature the monster is grappling. Bonus actions, reactions, and legendary actions with a save or attack roll carry the same fields. The script reads these from the text and its `RIDER_OVERRIDES` and `DRAINS` tables cover the wording it cannot read (swallowing, second failures, the Rug of Smothering). The app reads custom monsters the same way (`src/core/stat_block_reader.cpp`).

A helpful action has `benefit`: `tempHp` and `healing` dice, `advantageOnAttacks`, `acBonus`, and `until` (`sourceStart` or `sourceEnd`). The script and the custom monster reader find it in wording like "one creature of its choice ... gains 16 (2d10 + 5) Temporary Hit Points and has Advantage on attack rolls until the start of the giant's next turn".

A trait that changes attack rolls has `attackModifier`: `mode` (`advantage` or `disadvantage`), `when` (`selfBloodied`, `targetHurt`, `afterDamage` with `damageType`, or empty for a tick box worded by `ask`), and `sticky`, `meleeOnly`, `alliesToo`, `whileActive`. Custom monsters read it from the trait's text and the Effects editor sets it.

Damage parts are tagged `always`, `advantage` (added when the attack had Advantage), `advantageAlt` and `alternative` (replace the part before them), `conditional` (extra damage), or `ongoing` (listed, never rolled with the hit). An `alternative` or `conditional` part's `if` says when it applies: `@targetBloodied`, `@selfBloodied`, and `@grappledBySelf` the app checks itself; anything else is the stat block's words, shown as a tick box on the action. In the custom monster editor's damage line it is written after the part: `1d6 piercing conditional if the boar moved 20+ feet straight toward it`.
